## 1. Topic Statement

My curation project focuses on how AI can make our daily lives a little easier, especially by keeping us organized, providing resources, and helping us find ways to live a more productive and healthy life. I'm also interested in the practical ways we already use AI. I also want to be realistic when it comes to a topic like this, so I will be including both the helpful sides of AI and the risk/limitations that come from using it. In the end, I'm not suggesting that AI should replace experts or anything, but to show how much of a tool it can be. As long as we use it for good and important things. 


---

## 2. Proposed Organizational Structure

I've decided to organize the collection based on real-life situations instead of technical AI categories, because most people don't care what kind of AI they should use; they just want simple solutions to everyday problems. For each source, I'll give a generalized categories and add some tags so it can be easily found by anyone. 

### Categories

- **Learning & School**
  - Studying
  - Understanding difficult ideas
  - Research
  - Responsible student use

- **Productivity & Work**
  - Planning
  - Writing
  - Brainstorming
  - Workplace tasks
  - Career information

- **Health & Wellness**
  - Health information
  - Safety
  - Healthcare questions
  - Responsible AI use

- **Public/Media Information**
  - Public statistics 
  - AI trends 
  - Media and Public opinions

- **Safety & Digital Awareness**
  - Deepfakes
  - AI scams
  - Impersonation

I chose this structure because it lets a reader quickly find information based on what they are trying to do.

### Annotation 

**What it covers:** The Pew Research Center Article looks at how people interact with their everyday lives, including chatbots and AI that shows up in areas such as social media, healthcare, and financial services.
**Why it is valuable:** It is important because it gives my project real survey data instead of opinions and helps establish the common AI we have already.

## Source 2: UNESCO — “Guidance for Generative AI in Education and Research”

**URL:**
https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research

**Category Placement:** Learning & School -> Responsible Use

### Annotation 

**What it covers:** UNESCO talks about how generative Ai can be used in educational settings and research. But it also talks about how they address ethics, privacy, and human-centered use.
**Why it is valuable:** It's important because it shows that AI isn't a shortcut, but it shows how people can use it responsibly.
**Who would benefit:** This can benefit students, teachers, and anyone who uses AI for learning and research purposes.
**Limitations:** The information is pretty broad and focuses more on education policy, and doesn't give intructions for all of the AI tools.

## Source 3: International Labour Organization — “Generative AI and Jobs: A 2025 Update”

**URL:**
https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/2025-05/Research%20brief_FINAL_15May2025_21.05.25_1.pdf

**Category Placement:** Productivity & Work -> Jobs

### Annotation 

**What it covers:** ILO explains how generative AI may affect different occupations by looking at the tasks in hand and levels of exposure and gives us a chart showing it.
**Why it is valuable:** It gives a more realistic view of AI at work instead of assuming AI will simply take over and get rid of all of the jobs.
**Who would benefit:** Students, workers and people thinking about their future careers.
**Limitations:** It is more of a global occupational analysis, so other workplaces can look at or experience AI very differently.

## Source 4: Federal Trade Commission — “AI Impersonation Protections”

**URL:**
https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/02/ftc-proposes-new-protections-combat-ai-impersonation-individuals

**Category Placement:** Safety & Digital Awareness -> Scams

### Annotation 

**What it covers:** The Federal Trade Commission discusses how AI enabled impersonation fraud, including voice cloning and deepfakes, and talks about the efforts to strengthen their protection to combat scams like that.
**Why it is valuable:** It shows us not only what AI can do for people, but it also shows that the same AI software can be misused.
**Who would benefit:** Every internet user who wants to recognize scams and prevent them.
**Limitations:** It focuses more on consumer protection instead of teaching the consumer how to use some of the AI tools.

## Source 5: World Health Organization — “Artificial Intelligence for Health”

**URL:**
https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/artificial-intelligence-for-health

**Category Placement:** Health & Wellness > Responsible AI

### Annotation 

**What it covers:** The World Health Organization talks about the potential of AI to support health systems while talking about its safety and ethical use. It tells us that AI can help address challenges like a workforce employment shortage and things of that nature. 
**Why it is valuable:** Health is a big thing in our society where people can rely on AI too much, so this source reminds us that humans experts are still there and need oversight.
**Who would benefit:** Anyone who is interested in AI and health information.
**Limitations:** It focuses more on health systems and policies, not things like medical diagnosis or treatment.
